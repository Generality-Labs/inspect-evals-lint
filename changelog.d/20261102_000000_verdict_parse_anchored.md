### Added

- `verdict_parse_anchored` (IEBP015) fails where a judge's verdict is read by substring (`"yes" in reply`, `"answer: no" in reply`) or by an unanchored regex whose pattern is a verdict word, an alternation of them such as `(yes|no)`, or a one-character choice such as `[01]`, with at most a label before it. The words are a fixed vocabulary: yes, no, y, n, true, false, correct, incorrect, pass and fail. Each of these matches the token inside other words and anywhere in the judge's reasoning, so a right grade becomes a wrong one on ordinary judge prose. An anchor (`^`, `$`, `\A`, `\Z`, `\b`, `\B` or a lookaround) passes; text read from `state.output` is the model under test's and is not checked. The rule also warns on a search over the whole text for a labelled number such as `Ratio:\s*(\d+)`, which can read a line the model under test wrote and a judge or checker quoted ([#57](https://github.com/Generality-Labs/inspect-evals-lint/issues/57)).
